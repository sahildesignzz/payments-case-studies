1

Before checkout

One button, many verbs HIG

Apple ships the button in fixed verbs (buy, check out, book, donate, subscribe, top up…), so the label says what will happen, not just “pay”. The variant with the user’s own card on it tells them, before they tap, which card will be charged.

Rules: use the button only to pay or to set up Apple Pay; never hide it or show it as unavailable. If a size or colour is missing, explain that after the tap.

our note This is the same idea as Wise’s “button prompt” (see Wise, “The small parts”): a dead button never tells the user what to do next.

Evidence Apple’s own design rules. Guidance, not a measured result. Apple HIG · Apple Pay

2

Placement

First, same size, never buried A

HIG: “If Apple Pay is available, assume people want to use it.” Put it first, at least as large as other buttons, and on product pages, not just at the end of checkout.

Stripe found that showing Apple Pay early (Express Checkout Element) averaged 2× conversion compared with showing it at the end of checkout. In an A/B test at Wish, defaulting to Apple Pay for users who had it set up gave a “2X higher conversion rate increase” (Stripe’s wording; unclear whether 2× the rate or 2× the lift).

Evidence Stripe holdback test across 50+ methods (A); placement studies not described in detail. Stripe blog, Apr 2025

Evidence Wish A/B on defaulting (B). Stripe Apple Pay best practices

3

Case study with screens · Meta.com

Moving the wallets above the form B

  1. 01
    Express payment visibility: wallets moved from “Prefer PayPal? Choose it on the next step” (old, bottom) to the top of the page.
  2. 02
    Form hierarchy: fields grouped by task, labels stay visible once filled.
  3. 03
    Order summary: the total moves from the bottom of the form into a collapsible header.
  4. 04
    Contextual errors: the old banner (“There were 2 problems…”) becomes a message on the field (“ZIP needs 5 digits”).
  5. 05
    Mobile usability: bigger fields and a larger primary button.

The 01–05 markers are the designer’s; we matched each to the five changes listed in the same order on the page.

A senior product designer on Meta.com’s checkout names five changes. The first is the Apple Pay lesson in one line: “Wallets moved above the form, so returning buyers can pay before typing anything.” The others: fields grouped by task, the total kept in a collapsible header, errors beside the field, 48–52px tap targets.

Result reported: +5.4% purchase rate from Apple Pay Express Checkout within the first 24 hours.

our note 24 hours is a very short window and no sample size is given. It agrees in direction with Stripe’s tests, but on its own it proves little. The screens are the real value here.

Evidence Designer’s portfolio, insider number, no method (B). Jeffrey Lewis, Meta.com checkout redesign

4

The moment of paying

The sheet: what you buy, from whom, with what HIG

  1. 1
    Who is paid, and how much: “Pay Food Truck $34.87”, largest text on the sheet.
  2. 2
    The card itself, shown as the card art, with “Change Payment Method” under it.
  3. 3
    Card row: name, amount, and the cash-back the card earns.
  4. 4
    Line items: subtotal and “Due Today”. HIG: show only what’s essential.
  5. 5
    One physical action: “Confirm with Side Button”. No form, no card number, no CVV.

HIG: use the contact, shipping and payment details Apple Pay already holds instead of stored ones; ask for size, colour, gift notes and pickup store before the sheet opens, so the sheet only confirms.

our note Compare with Meta’s old form above: twelve fields vs. one button press. That gap is what the conversion numbers above are measuring.

Evidence Apple’s design rules (guidance). Apple HIG · Apple Pay

5

Trust

Every charge named, including future ones HIG

  1. 1
    Gift wrap +$5.00 and coupon −$2.00 are separate lines, so the total explains itself.
  2. 2
    “$9.99 per month · Starting Today · Ending December 8, 2026”: the recurring promise is stated before you agree.
  3. 3
    The trial is the headline: $0.00 today. The later $22.00/month and its start date sit right under it.
  4. 4
    When the final amount isn’t known (weighed groceries), the sheet says so: “Amount Pending” instead of a fake total.

our note The same principle as Wise’s calculator (see Wise, “The price, on the homepage”): show the full cost, and how it is built, while the user can still say no.

Evidence Apple’s design rules (guidance). Apple HIG · Apple Pay

6

Failure and recovery

Fix it in place, never start again HIG

  1. 1
    Apple: the bad address turns red in the sheet; the user never leaves the payment to fix it.
  2. 2
    The confirm button becomes the fix: “Update Shipping Address”, an action, not a dead end.
  3. 3
    Meta: “No charge was made” first (the fear), then two ways forward. Apple Pay is offered as the escape route.

Meta’s three failure rules, quoted: “The message sits on the one field at fault; every other value stays as entered.” · “One explanation, two ways forward: retry the card or move to another method.” · “Submit and payment fields lock until a response, so a second tap cannot send a second charge.”

our note This answers round 1’s biggest gap (no source on failure flows). It is still design rationale, not measured: neither source reports what these states did to completion.

Evidence Apple HIG (guidance) and Meta designer’s portfolio (no data for these states). Jeffrey Lewis, Meta.com

7

After paying

Ask for an account only after the money moved HIG

  1. 1
    Confirmation first: “Thank you for your order! Order #123456”.
  2. 2
    Only now: “Create Account” or “Sign Up with Apple”, pre-filled from the order. HIG: don’t require an account before purchase.

Stripe’s best-practice doc says the same: remove forced sign-up, and ask for a password after payment, on the confirmation page.

our note Same move as Revolut payroll (removing a forced dependency) and Wise (KYC after the quote): the hard ask comes after the value.

Evidence Apple HIG and Stripe guidance (no data). Stripe Apple Pay best practices

8

News · iOS 27

Choosing a card becomes a swipe D

Reported for iOS 27: the payment sheet lets people swipe between cards or tap a card to see a grid of all cards, and can show rewards, balances and pay-later options before approving. Under iOS 26, tapping the card “often opened delivery-address settings rather than card selection” (compare the payment sheet above).

our note No screenshots of the new sheet were found in a source we could verify, so none are shown. The article’s own warning is worth keeping: rewards and pay-later offers on the sheet could turn a calm payment layer into advertising.

Evidence Tech news article, Sep 2026 (D). Not checked against Apple’s documentation. SquaredTech
